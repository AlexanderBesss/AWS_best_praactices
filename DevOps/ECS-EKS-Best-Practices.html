ECS/EKS Best Practices

Container orchestration is the backbone of modern cloud-native infrastructure, enabling automated deployment, scaling, and management of containerized workloads at scale. Choosing the right orchestrator on AWS directly impacts operational complexity, cost efficiency, and your team's ability to respond to changing demand — this guide covers the key decisions and patterns for ECS and EKS so you can match infrastructure to workload requirements.

Choosing ECS vs EKS

Amazon ECS and Amazon EKS both offer fully managed control planes, but they differ in ecosystem, networking model, and operating cost. The right choice depends on your team's expertise, portability needs, and tolerance for Kubernetes complexity. For broader patterns around running containerized workloads, see also Compute & Deployment Patterns.

Amazon ECS

AWS-native orchestrator with no control-plane charge and a lower learning curve. Best for AWS-centric teams that want a simpler orchestrator, fast time-to-market, and built-in service connectivity via ECS Service Connect.

Amazon EKS

Full managed Kubernetes with the broadest ecosystem: Helm charts, operators, Istio/Linkerd/Cilium, and multi-cloud portability. Best for teams that require Kubernetes APIs — in exchange for a per-cluster control-plane charge and a steeper learning curve.

Kubernetes version support

Understanding the version lifecycle is essential to avoid unexpected upgrade pressure and extended-support charges.

Support TierDurationCoverage
Standard support14 months from releaseControl plane, EKS-published AMIs, add-ons
Extended supportNext 12 months (26 months total)Control plane patches, critical AMI/add-on patches (additional cost)
Warning

Upgrade clusters before standard support ends to avoid the extended-support surcharge. If extended support is disabled, EKS automatically upgrades the control plane when standard support ends; after extended support ends, EKS also automatically upgrades unsupported control planes.

Warning

For new ECS workloads, evaluate ECS Service Connect; for EKS, evaluate a supported Kubernetes service-mesh or networking project. Existing App Mesh users should migrate before the end-of-support date.

Reliability and Deployment Practices

These practices apply to both ECS and EKS and pair well with a solid delivery pipeline — see CI/CD Best Practices for deployment automation guidance.

  • Spread replicas across Availability Zones and use topology-spread constraints or anti-affinity where a zonal failure must not remove all capacity.
  • Configure readiness, liveness, and startup checks for their distinct purposes. Avoid making a shallow liveness check depend on every downstream service, which can amplify an outage.
  • For ECS rolling deployments, configure the deployment circuit breaker and rollback; for EKS, combine rollout health with PodDisruptionBudgets and sensible surge/unavailable settings.
  • Run more than one task or pod for highly available services, drain connections on termination, and allow enough stop/termination grace time.
  • Use immutable image tags or digests, scan images, run as a non-root user where possible, use read-only filesystems, and drop unnecessary Linux capabilities.
  • Prefer EKS access entries for cluster access and EKS Pod Identity or IRSA for workload credentials; do not distribute static AWS keys to containers.
