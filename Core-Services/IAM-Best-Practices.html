IAM Best Practices

IAM is the foundation of AWS security, controlling who can access your resources and what actions they can perform. Without proper IAM configuration, a single compromised credential can lead to full account takeover, data breaches, and excessive costs.

Core principles

These principles shape every other recommendation on this page: keep permissions minimal, prefer temporary credentials over long-lived ones, and review access continuously.

Least privilege framework

Warning

Over-provisioned permissions are the most common path to account compromise. Ensure every identity has only the access it genuinely needs.

PrincipleDescriptionImplementation
Least PrivilegeGrant only permissions required for the taskUse permission policies, not AdministratorAccess
Permission BoundariesSet maximum permissions an entity can receiveIAM permission boundaries on roles/users
Just-in-Time AccessTime-bounded elevation when neededIAM Identity Center with a temporary elevated-access workflow, or a vetted external identity governance system
Regular ReviewAudit and remove unused permissionsIAM Access Analyzer, credential reports

Effective permissions are the intersection of every applicable policy. An explicit deny anywhere in the chain wins outright; otherwise an applicable allow is required.

Choosing the right identity type

Choosing the right identity type reduces long-term credential risk and simplifies access management.

Entity TypeUse CaseBest Practice
IAM UsersExceptional human or workload cases requiring long-term credentialsPrefer IAM Identity Center or federation for people and IAM roles/temporary credentials for workloads

MFA enforcement

Tip

MFA is the single most effective control against credential theft. Even with stolen passwords, attackers cannot bypass a properly enforced second factor.

MFA requirements

ScopeRequirementImplementation
Root userAWS-requiredRegister a passkey/security key where possible; all standalone, management, and member account root users must configure MFA unless member root credentials are centrally removed
IAM UsersStrongly recommendedPrefer federation; where IAM users remain, require MFA for console access and sensitive actions
Privileged accessStrongly recommendedEnforce MFA in the identity provider/Identity Center. Use aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent only where the request context supports it
CI/CD RolesNot requiredMachine-to-machine, use IAM roles
Service RolesNot requiredEC2, Lambda, etc. use role assumption

MFA condition keys

These keys let you enforce MFA at the policy level, ensuring sensitive operations require re-authentication.

Condition KeyDescriptionExample
aws:MultiFactorAuthPresentRequires MFA for the requestBool: true
aws:MultiFactorAuthAgeTime since MFA authenticationNumericLessThan: 3600

Role design

Trust policies

Trust policies define who can assume a role. Overly permissive trust policies are a primary vector for cross-account attacks and privilege escalation.

Trusted EntityUse CaseExample
AWS ServiceEC2, Lambda, ECS assume roleService: ec2.amazonaws.com
AWS AccountCross-account accessAWS: arn:aws:iam::123456789012:root
IAM User/RoleSpecific user/role assumptionAWS: arn:aws:iam::123456789012:role/RoleName
External IDThird-party access with extra securityCondition: sts:ExternalId
Federated UserSAML/OIDC identity providersFederated: arn:aws:iam::account:saml-provider/Provider

Session duration

Longer sessions increase the window of exposure if credentials are compromised. Matching session length to actual need limits blast radius.

SessionDefaultConfigurable Range / LimitRecommendation
IAM role assumed through STS1 hour when DurationSeconds is omittedThe role's maximum session duration is configurable from 1-12 hours; AssumeRole* requests can generally ask for 15 minutes up to that maximumUse the shortest duration needed; role chaining is capped at 1 hour
SAML or web-identity role session1 hour when a duration is not supplied15 minutes up to the role's configured maximum (at most 12 hours), subject to the identity-provider assertion/tokenPrefer purpose-built federation such as IAM Identity Center or Amazon Cognito where applicable
IAM Identity Center account role session1 hour1-12 hours per permission setUse shorter sessions for sensitive environments and just-in-time elevated access

Do not treat all temporary credentials as having the same lifetime. Other STS operations differ: for example, GetFederationToken and IAM-user GetSessionToken can issue credentials for up to 36 hours, while credentials requested with root user credentials are limited to one hour.

AWS IAM Identity Center

IAM Users accumulate stale permissions and lack centralized lifecycle management. Identity Center solves these problems at scale.

IAM users vs Identity Center

Credential management

Warning

Long-lived credentials are the highest risk in any AWS account. Understanding credential lifetimes helps you minimize exposure windows.

Credential rotation checklist

AWS Organizations & SCPs

Warning

SCPs set the maximum available permissions for principals in member accounts; they do not grant permissions. They do not restrict the management account, and they do not restrict service-linked roles.

SCP types

Understanding the difference between allow and deny SCPs is critical for designing effective organizational guardrails without over-restricting workloads.

TypeDescriptionUse Case
Allow List SCPExplicitly allow specific services/actionsRestrict to approved services only
Deny List SCPDeny specific services/actionsBlock dangerous operations
Baseline SCPApplied to all accountsOrganization-wide guardrails

Common SCP patterns

These patterns represent the most frequently needed organizational guardrails, preventing the highest-impact mistakes across all member accounts.

SCPPurposeActions Affected
Deny IAM ChangesPrevent privilege escalationiam:DeleteUser, iam:AttachPolicy
Deny Region AccessData sovereigntyAll actions in non-approved regions
Deny Service TerminationPrevent accidental deletionec2:TerminateInstances, rds:DeleteDBInstance
Deny Public S3Prevent data exposurePrefer account-level S3 Block Public Access plus narrowly scoped policy guardrails; action-only denies cannot reliably classify every public policy
Deny Key DeletionProtect encryptionkms:ScheduleKeyDeletion — see KMS Best Practices
